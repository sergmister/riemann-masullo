# Outside reports on *The Music of the Primes*

This index follows the pages of A. M. Masullo’s 20 November 2026 draft and records what outside readers reported, the evidence or repairs they offered, and where the corresponding work stands in the Lean formalization. The reports are the work of the readers credited below. Each entry is a lead. Its inclusion carries no verdict on the report.

This is a snapshot built on **22 November 2026**. The project keeps [ERRATA.md](ERRATA.md) current. The **In ERRATA.md** column gives the entry number and its check status. A related entry concerns a different claim or step. The errata’s **reviewed** label records a check against the paper, and **Lean** records a machine-checked refutation independent of unproved statements. The Lean column identifies relevant declarations, some of which still have open proofs. The errata explains its check levels and what has been withdrawn or moved. The earlier combined entry R3 was moved after the project checked issue #2469 item by item. Its individual findings are cited below by their current entries where applicable.

## Sources

**Isaac Yonemoto.** His dependency audit began on 20 November 2026. It traces the dependencies of the introductory deduction and selected trace arguments, and describes a 2b4m kernel check of logical composition with opaque predicates and disclosed assumptions. The audit gives no model name. [Yonemoto’s audit](https://github.com/ityonemo/music-of-primes)

**Jenny Lorraine Nielsen.** Her November 2026 note, *A Failure of Uniform Boundedness in Masullo’s Proposed Proof of the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis*, gives two objections in prose. Yonemoto’s repository hosts the note and a 2b4m encoding of the two objections that its README says passes using ten classical citations. [Repository hosting Nielsen’s note](https://github.com/ityonemo/music-of-primes)

**Ben Golub and Refine Technologies.** Their *Report on The Music of the Primes* gives ten numbered objections and an appendix on the strip identities. The undated title page credits the Refine harness and “all frontier models”. The PDF metadata gives 21 November 2026 as its creation date. The report presents prose arguments. [Refine’s report](https://github.com/Refine-Technologies-Inc/music-of-primes-report-by-refine)

**The companion published by Aran Nayebi.** *Further repairs, attempted closures, and the remaining estimate* is a 57-page second companion dated 21 November 2026. The PDF has no personal byline, and the metadata describes the work as technical analysis prepared in a conversation. It gives prose proofs and attempted repairs, chiefly for the ordinary zeta function. Nayebi hosts the PDF independently of the Lean project. [Companion PDF](https://anayebi.github.io/human_grh_unresolved.pdf)

**Dev Goyal.** His 21 November 2026 audit comprises a preprint, a projection supplement, a detailed report, six verification dossiers and exact-arithmetic checks. The README credits GPT 6.2 Astra in Codex with generating the material, Claude Opus 5.5 with checking the proofs, and Goyal with directing the work. It reports no human review. The scripts check selected finite arithmetic identities. The source locators below use the report’s and dossiers’ own headings. [Goyal’s audit](https://github.com/dvgyl/masullo-grh-proof-audit)

**Eric Naslund.** His review comprises three reports dated 20 November 2026 on the trace argument, with an attempted repair for the ordinary zeta function and indexes of the manuscript’s pages and lines. Naslund organized and prompted the work. The README credits Codex GPT Astra 6.2 and two GPT Pro conversations. [Naslund’s review](https://github.com/enaslund/rh-verification)

**Issue #2469.** @elmismisimoxhunca posted an audit of Chapters 11–12 on 21 November 2026. The author says they used GPT 6.2 Astra with extended reasoning to check page images and recompute examples, chiefly over ℚ with trivial character. The leads came from a Prove2Me discussion, mostly from Yi, with earlier objections from Calvin Yost-Wolff, lll and ghseeli among others. The issue gives no human number-theory or Lean verification. @leecrossley added a separate prime-two calculation that day. Item numbers below refer to the detailed findings in the issue. [Chapter 11–12 audit and comments](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/issues/2469)

**John Baez.** His 22 November 2026 post gives a general-reader account of the manuscript’s strategy and describes two reported problems. The detailed locators below come from the audits and reports. [*Masullo on the Riemann Hypothesis*](https://johncarlosbaez.wordpress.com/2026/11/22/masullo-on-the-riemann-hypothesis/)

## Reports without a paper page and limits of the checks

The companion also tests routes for ordinary RH and related arithmetic estimates that have no specific counterpart in the manuscript. These are results and proposals in the companion. They have no corresponding entries in the errata or statements in the Lean library.

- **§3.5, pp. 26–27.** The prime-box sums `M_X(y)=Σ_{d≤y,d∣Q_X}μ(d)`, where `Q_X=∏_{p≤X}p`, cannot satisfy a uniform `O(y^θ)` bound for `θ<1`. The proposed bound continues the limit to `Re z>θ`, while `E_X(c)→0` for `max(θ,0)<c<1`.
- **§4.5, pp. 32–33.** Translates with amplitude `e^{−bL}`, `L=log λ`, tend to zero in unweighted norms and can have a nonzero Fourier limit at `ib`. The semilocal route still needs weighted convergence and its lowest-eigenvalue comparison.
- **§§5.1–5.6, pp. 33–37, and §§6.6, 9.1, pp. 43–44, 54–55.** The reported mollifier power is `2β_*−1`. Fixed taper orders preserve it, while order `⌈log N⌉` changes the reciprocal being approximated. At `P=30, p=7`, the prime-box cross term `∫₀∞F₃₀(y)F₃₀(y/7)dy/y²` is below `−7/1000`. The reported 7/8 zero-free half-plane is a conditional input giving `J_d(X)≪X^{3/4}` and `N_N≪_εN^{3/4+ε}/log²N`. These criteria require subpower bounds.
- **§§6.1–6.5, pp. 37–43, and §§7.4, 8.4, 9.1, pp. 47–48, 54–55.** The companion gives dyadic prime-energy and one-sided covariance criteria for RH. For `D(x)=ψ(2x)/(2x)−ψ(x)/x`, the Mellin transform has residue `−m_ρ(2^{ρ−1}−1)/ρ` at each nontrivial zero. A fixed compact correction cannot cancel those poles. The covariance balance is `R(X)+E(X)²/X=1+Q(X)+2C(X)`. Cauchy–Schwarz leaves an error of order `N log(2N)`. A dyadic contraction relates the weighted norms, while `d₀(y)=y^{−1/4}` shows the gap between `dy/y` and `dy` estimates. In the prime-pair kernel the continuous density term cancels and the signed difference of the two discrete masses, each of order `X`, remains uncontrolled at subpower scale.
- **§§7.1–7.3, 7.5 and 8.1–8.3, pp. 44–47, 48–54.** The factorial inversion keeps a leading floor term `σ(t)log(t)/(4t)` that reconstructs the original discrepancy. The signed inverse has diagonal energy `(6/π²)∥b∥₂² log X+O(1)`, with a primitive-pair sum still to bound. A continuous countermodel to a Selberg-symmetry shortcut yields covariance of order `√X`. A shifted completed-zeta modulus inequality at fixed shift `h` would exclude precisely the zeros to the right of `1/2+h`. In the dilation Gram test, a dense block of `O(N)` indices gives a quadratic form of order `N²`; the available weighted estimate leaves `O_ε(X^{1−ε})`.
