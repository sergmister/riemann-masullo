# What a physical window remembers about rational theta

Masullo defines a closed space **after** multiplying by a physical cutoff on p. 480. The Paley–Wiener projection on p. 488 uses the ambient Sobolev norm. Section 12.3 on p. 514 leaves its global-to-local comparison unwritten. These operations can be distinguished by an explicit test in the trivial character over $\mathbb Q$. Normalize the radial Haar measure to $dx/x$ after extracting the norm-one volume, put $x=|g|$, $t=\log x$, and write $D(t)=e^{2\alpha |t|}(1+t^2)^{s/2}$. The positive-width use of the fixed real Fourier band below is a proposed comparison for §12.3. ## Exact interpolation at a fixed Fourier band

Take an even real-place Schwartz function $f$ with $f(0)=\widehat f(0)=0$ and $\widehat f$ supported on $[-B,B]$. With finite components $1_{\mathbb Z_p}$, the actual rational theta is $2\sum_{n\ge1} f(nx)$. The homogeneous theta of Chapter 10 adds the norm-one Tamagawa mass. As $B$ varies, these tests generate a dense subset of the actual trivial isotype of the weighted geometric space. This holds on every compact interval of weights and at every Sobolev order $s\ge0$. [Adelic series](RationalThetaSeries.lean) · [Density with both polar conditions](RationalThetaSpan.lean)

More generally, replace the integer indicator by the unit indicator at the primes dividing a squarefree $P$ and put $P=1$ for the original test. After multiplying by $\sqrt{x}$, Poisson summation and inclusion–exclusion give
$$
 H_P(f)(x)=2\sqrt{x}\sum_{\substack{n\ge1\\(n,P)=1}}f(nx)
 =2x^{-1/2}\sum_{m\ge1}c_P(m)\widehat f\!\left(\frac{m}{Px}\right),\qquad
 c_P(m)=\frac{\mu(P)}{P}\prod_{p\mid(P,m)}(1-p).
$$
Indeed the term for a divisor $d$ before regrouping has coefficient $\mu(d)/d$ and frequency $l/(dx)$, $l\ge1$. In particular $c_P(1)\ne0$. The profile vanishes for $x\le1/(PB)$. [Finite-prime dilation identity](PrimeSieveStep.lean)

Fix $U>0$. **Every smooth target compactly supported in $(1/(PB),\infty)$ can be matched exactly on the whole interval $0<x\le U$.** To see this, use $\xi=1/(Px)\ge a=1/(PU)$ and express the target there as $2x^{-1/2}c_P(1)g(\xi)$. The reciprocal profile has a smooth even extension supported inside $[-B,B]$, zero near the origin. Only $m\le B/a$ can contribute. After dividing by $c_P(1)$ the equation is $(I+N)h=g$, where $Nh(\xi)$ is a finite combination of $h(m\xi)$, $m\ge2$. The finite alternating inverse gives an even smooth solution $h$ supported in the same band since $N^k$ vanishes on this window when $2^ka\ge B$. Subtract $r h(r\,\cdot)$ with $r=1+B/a$ to make its integral zero while preserving every observed value. Inverse Fourier transformation produces $f$. Lean checks the recursion for arbitrary dilation coefficients, the integer Poisson formula for $P=1$, and its identification with the actual rational homogeneous theta including its Haar factor. The extension to squarefree $P$ uses the displayed divisor calculation. [Finite dilation inverse](FiniteWindowInterpolation.lean) · [Adelic interpolation](RationalWindow.lean)

For any bounded cutoff $\rho$ supported in $x\le U$, the closed span of the profiles $\rho H_P(f)$ in $L^2(D\,dx/x)$ is therefore the whole subspace supported on $\{\rho\ne0,\ x>1/(PB)\}$, up to null sets. Smooth compactly supported targets are dense above the threshold, and the closure of the multiplication range is the subspace supported where the multiplier is nonzero. Its orthogonal projection is multiplication by the indicator. In particular a smooth convolution supported strictly above norm one, compressed to a bounded physical annulus in this subspace, has trace zero. The localized convolution is trace class by smoothness on a compact rectangle and its diagonal is zero. One may also first apply a smooth annular cutoff and then use cyclicity for the bounded multipliers. This scalar conclusion concerns a specified order of operations. The project had already refuted a strict-subsequence assertion for the zero-line construction on p. 480. [Zero-line refutation](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/blob/256bf41783ff458dc4d8f8e70d7fa548ad81a2dc/RiemannMasullo/Ch11_ShellStructure/Sec1b_Printed.lean#L980)

## The cost of extending a profile

For a critical-line zeta zero $1/2+i\gamma$, the functional $\Lambda_\gamma(F)=\int_\mathbb R F(t)e^{i\gamma t}\,dt$ kills the global half-twisted theta space. On generators the Mellin transform at $z$ is $2\zeta(1/2+z)M_f(1/2+z)$ times $\prod_{p\mid P}(1-p^{-1/2-z})$, with $M_f(w)=\int_0^\infty f(x)x^w\,dx/x$. The identity follows first in the domain of absolute convergence and then by analytic continuation. The functional extends to the closed space for $\alpha>0$, $s\ge0$, since $D^{-1}$ is integrable. A zero with $14.13<\gamma<14.14$ already suffices. [Verified critical-line zero](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/blob/256bf41783ff458dc4d8f8e70d7fa548ad81a2dc/RiemannMasullo/External/ZetaLab/HardyFirstZero/FirstZero.lean#L106) · [Annihilation of the global image](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/blob/256bf41783ff458dc4d8f8e70d7fa548ad81a2dc/RiemannMasullo/Ch10_TraceSpectral/Sec2.lean#L2916)

Choose a nonnegative smooth log-norm bump $b$ of positive integral, supported above the threshold, and prescribe the physical vector $G(t)=e^{-i\gamma t}b(t)$. Write $\mu=\int b$. If a global extension $F$ agrees with $G$ on $t\le R$ and $R\ge0$ is above the support, then the moment of its upper tail is $-\mu$. Cauchy–Schwarz gives
$$
 \|\mathbf1_{t>R}F\|_{L^2(D\,dt)}
 \ \ge\ \frac{|\mu|}{\sqrt{\int_R^\infty D(t)^{-1}\,dt}}
 \ \ge\ |\mu|\sqrt{2\alpha}\,e^{\alpha R}(1+R^2)^{s/4}.
$$
For matching only on $|t|\le R$, both tails can pay the cost and the second constant becomes $\sqrt{\alpha}$. Approximate matching in the ambient norm still forces the full lift norms to diverge when the observed profiles approach $G$. The estimate uses one moment and gives a lower bound for every actual lift. [Weighted moment and tail bounds](WindowMellinTail.lean) · [Ambient lift estimate](WindowRestriction.lean)

## A one-sided estimate for adding a prime

Let $p\nmid P$ and denote the global norm-closed band space by $U_{P,B}$ and its orthogonal projector by $Q_{P,B}$. For the half twist, $T_pF(x)=p^{-1/2}F(px)$ and $H_{Pp}(f)=(1-T_p)H_P(f)$. Also $T_pH_P(f)=H_P(f(p\,\cdot))$, whose Fourier band is at most $pB$. Thus for $N\ge1$ the first $N$ sieved terms of a profile in $U_{P,B}$ lie in $U_{Pp,p^{N-1}B}$. Summing the finite geometric series recovers $u-T_p^N u$ in that larger space. The closest-point property gives
$$
 \|(I-Q_{Pp,p^{N-1}B})Q_{P,B}\|
 \le \|T_p^N\|
 \le p^{-N(1/2-\alpha)}(1+N\log p)^{s/2}.
$$
Multiplication by a nuclear operator of trace norm $C$ gives a trace error bounded by $C$ times this quantity for inserting the larger sieved projection on the smaller band's range. Directions contributed by the larger space require separate control for an estimate of the difference of its full trace. For $0\le\alpha<1/2$ this gives recovery as the relative bandwidth increases. At fixed physical cutoff, letting both absolute bands tend to infinity gives equal adjacent traces for this fixed prime since their unbanded closed spaces agree. The nonzero prime contribution previously found in a drifting lower window uses a different ordering. ## The operator that supplies the missing information

Let $Q$ project onto the global geometric theta space $G$, $P=I-Q$, and let $E$ multiply by the indicator of a bounded window. The restriction $J=E|_G$, with its domain carrying the global norm, satisfies $JJ^*=EQE$. For a smooth radial convolution with log kernel $k$ and cutoff $\rho$ supported in $I$, enlarge the window to contain $I\cup(I-\operatorname{supp}k)$. Then $T=M_\rho A_\phi=ETE$. Cyclicity yields the exact identity
$$
 \operatorname{Tr}(TQ)=\operatorname{Tr}(T\,JJ^*).
$$
The product of the restriction and its adjoint retains information that its ordinary closed range loses. At $\alpha=1/2,\ s\ge3$, a nonzero nonnegative convolution test supported in $3/2<x<7/4$ has strictly negative whole geometric trace. Its normalized integrand is $\phi(x)x^{-1/2}((x^2-1)^{-1}-x)$ against $dx/x$. This supplies a sign check for any proposed arithmetic computation of $JJ^*$. [Window restriction identity](WindowRestriction.lean) · [Wide-strip comparison](https://github.com/sergmister/riemann-masullo/blob/f876605de446d2f8d475c2c4b2a4ca81ff5cd5c6/RiemannMasullo/Research/StripNuclearity/RationalPrimeFreeDiagonal.lean)

There is also a quantitative obstruction to recovering this operator by the ordinary window projection. Put $v_\gamma(t)=e^{-i\gamma t}D(t)^{-1}/\sqrt{\int D^{-1}}$. It is a unit vector with $Pv_\gamma=v_\gamma$. For exhausting windows the norm of $EPE$, the difference between $E$ and $EQE$, tends to one. On any fixed window of positive measure, its distance to the compact operators is at least $\eta=\int_E D^{-1}/\int D^{-1}$. Indeed Hardy's theorem and isolation of the zeros of the meromorphic zeta function give critical-line ordinates tending to infinity. The unit vectors $w_\gamma=Ev_\gamma/\sqrt{\eta}$ tend weakly to zero by the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma, while $\langle EPEw_\gamma,w_\gamma\rangle=\|P w_\gamma\|^2\ge|\langle w_\gamma,v_\gamma\rangle|^2=\eta$. Each compact operator takes this sequence to zero in norm. Lean checks the exhausting-window norm theorem. The fixed-window essential-norm bound is the analytic implication just given. [Compression norm](WindowRestriction.lean) · [Hardy’s theorem on upstream main](https://github.com/mccorvie/riemann-masullo/pull/7292)

There is a direct way to compute the surviving finite compressions without zero locations. For linearly independent physical theta profiles $F_1,\ldots,F_d$ form the two finite matrices
$$
 G_{ij}=\int\overline{F_i(t)}F_j(t)D(t)\,dt,\qquad
 C_{ij}=\int\overline{F_i(t)}(TF_j)(t)D(t)\,dt .
$$
The trace of $T$ times their ambient span projection is $\operatorname{tr}(G^{-1}C)$, by writing that projection as $U(U^*U)^{-1}U^*$ for the column operator $U$. For a given finite family the positive support thresholds have a common positive lower bound. Above that bound the two integer theta sums in each entry of $G$ can be expanded and integrated term by term by Schwartz decay. Below it the summed profiles vanish. The same method applies to $C$ on the compact input-output rectangle. Increasing finite spans with dense union give the true fixed-cutoff trace. This exhibits where a comparison using the $S$-local integral must reproduce the global Gram matrix, even though both inputs to $C$ are observed in a bounded window.

The all-field finite-stage approximation recovers the global trace by increasing the genuine additive Fourier boxes at each fixed physical cutoff, then exhausting the physical cutoff. The concrete target for §12.3 is to compute the trace of the **smoothed** windowed projection from the $S$-local data, retaining the global extension norm, and recover the negative wide-strip value before varying the width. Compact or finite-rank approximation of the unsmoothed difference in operator norm would fail on each nonempty window. The trace pairing can still converge. Identifying its value with the local principal values and two pole terms at smaller widths is the outstanding arithmetic comparison.
